Dryfall should have died years ago. The wells ran bitter, the railroad skipped it, and the mapmakers scratched it off as a mercy. But tonight the town glows under a swollen red moon, its saloons loud again, its streets thick with drifters, bounty hunters, and silk-dressed strangers who don’t cast reflections in the window glass. Word has spread fast: a train that never stops anywhere has stopped here, its iron sides etched with symbols no preacher will name. Whatever it carries has made Dryfall worth killing for.

You arrive as the first gunshots crack. A gang known as the Hollow Spurs is already tearing through the depot, gunning down anyone between them and the train’s cargo. At the same time, a veiled mystic called Sister Caldera gathers a circle in the dirt, insisting the train must not be opened—that it is a coffin, a prison, and a promise all at once. The sheriff is missing, the deputy is drunk, and a well-dressed gambler named Lucien Voss is offering large sums of money to anyone willing to help him claim what’s inside before midnight.

As bodies fall and alliances form, the truth begins to bleed through: the train carries a bound entity, something ancient and furious that feeds on violence and grows stronger with every drop of blood spilled in its presence. The Hollow Spurs want to weaponize it. Voss wants to sell it. Sister Caldera wants it buried forever. And the longer the fighting continues, the louder something inside the train begins to laugh.

By dawn, Dryfall will either be empty again—or it will become the center of something far worse than a ghost town. Every choice draws blood, every deal has a cost, and under the red moon, even love can be a weapon or a weakness. The train doors are beginning to open.
