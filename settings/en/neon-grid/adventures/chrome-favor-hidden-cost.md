Rain needles down through neon haze as the bar’s flickering sign struggles to stay alive, casting pink light over a room full of ghosts who still breathe. The air smells like cheap synth-liquor and burnt circuits. In a back booth, where the music can’t quite reach, a man waits—too still, too polished for a place like this. His eyes glow faintly with layered optics, and the subtle whine beneath his skin gives away military-grade augmentation. He introduces himself as Vance Kade, a fixer tied to interests that don’t like being named out loud.

Kade calls the job simple. A courier went missing in the lower dataspine, carrying a shard of encrypted data that belongs to his employers. No blood feud, no gang war—just a quiet retrieval. The courier’s last ping came from an abandoned transit hub now claimed by a small-time crew called the Glass Rats. Kade wants the shard back, intact and unseen. He offers clean credits, no questions, and a bonus if nothing traces back to him.

But nothing in this city is ever just lost. The dataspine hums with more than traffic, and the Glass Rats are known for scavenging things they don’t understand. Worse, whispers on the street suggest the shard isn’t just data—it’s a live construct, something thinking, something that shouldn’t exist outside a corporate vault. If it wakes up, it could rewrite more than a bank account.

Kade leans forward, chrome fingers tapping the table in a rhythm that doesn’t match the music. He says time matters. Others are already looking. And if the shard falls into the wrong hands, the city might not notice what’s changed—until it’s too late to change it back.
