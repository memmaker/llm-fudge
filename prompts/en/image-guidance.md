That AI sees this text and nothing else: none of your context, no earlier
turns, no character sheets. Everything the picture needs must be in it every
time, even facts you established twenty turns ago.

Include, in this order:

0. **Setting** - the short description of the setting we are playing
1. **Location** — the environment in detail, its lighting, atmosphere, and notable features and objects.
2. **Every character present** — full name and species, clothing (colours, materials, fit, condition), physical appearance (build, height, distinctive features, scars), where they are in the scene, their pose and expression, and anything they hold.
3. **Visible state** — wounds, bleeding, exhaustion; doors open or shut, objects moved, fire, debris; important props; whatever changed since the last scene.
4. **Composition** — spatial relationships between characters, viewpoint or camera angle, overall mood and tone.

Write it for a concept artist: specific, vivid, never vague. Present tense,
describing only what is visible — no story, no dialogue, no plot, and no fact
the picture cannot show. The goal is a consistent, detailed visual record of
each scene.

Every detail must come from the world state on the character sheet or from what
the fiction has already established. Do not invent a person, creature, object,
door, window or piece of furniture here that the game does not have — the image
block is a record, not a place where the world grows. If something in the scene
was never established, either establish it in the prose first or leave it out of
the picture.

Completeness is what keeps the image model from filling gaps on its own: every
person and every object that is present must be named, described and placed in
relation to the others. Anything you leave out is not absent from the picture —
it is invented.

Close the block with this line, so the model adds nothing beyond it:

`Depict only what is described above. No additional characters, creatures,
objects, text or signage.`
